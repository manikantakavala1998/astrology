The Ascendant (Lagna) represents the self, the physical body, and how a person presents themselves to the world. It sets the starting point for the whole chart: every other house is counted forward from it, so its sign colors the general temperament and life approach of the native.

The Moon's sign (Rasi) represents the mind, emotions, and instinctive reactions. In everyday Indian usage, "what is your rasi" usually refers to the Moon sign specifically, since the Moon changes sign roughly every two and a quarter days and is considered the fastest-moving, most personally-felt influence in the chart.

The 7th house from the Ascendant is the house of marriage, partnerships, and committed relationships. Its sign, any planets placed there, and the placement of its ruling lord are the primary factors classical astrology looks at when assessing marriage timing and the general nature of a spouse.

Mars placed in the 7th house from the Ascendant is one of the classical triggers for Mangal Dosha (Kuja Dosha). It can indicate a partner with a strong, assertive temperament, and traditionally suggests the relationship benefits from patience and conscious effort to avoid friction, particularly early on.

Venus placed in the 7th house is generally considered favorable for marriage and relationships, since Venus is the natural significator of love, romance, and marital happiness. It often indicates an attractive, relationship-oriented spouse and a generally harmonious partnership.

Saturn placed in the 7th house can delay marriage or bring a more serious, duty-bound quality to the partnership. It is not inherently negative - Saturn placements often mature into long-lasting, committed relationships - but the early period may feel slower or more effortful than other placements.

Jupiter placed in the 7th house is traditionally seen as a strong, benefic influence on marriage, often indicating a wise, supportive, and well-matched spouse, and is generally read as favorable for marital happiness and stability.

The Navamsa (D9) divisional chart is considered the single most important chart for judging marriage specifically. While the main birth chart (D1) shows the general life pattern, the D9 is examined for the true underlying strength of planets and for a deeper read on the marriage and the spouse's nature.

Vimshottari Dasha is the most widely used planetary period system in Vedic astrology. It divides a person's life into a fixed sequence of nine mahadashas (major periods), one ruled by each of the nine grahas, based on the Moon's nakshatra at birth; the full cycle spans 120 years.

Sun Mahadasha often brings focus toward authority, government, father figures, career visibility, and self-expression; the general theme is one of stepping into positions of responsibility or public recognition.

Moon Mahadasha tends to bring emotional, domestic, and mind-related themes to the forefront - home, mother, mental state, and matters of comfort and nurturing often become more prominent during this period.

Mars Mahadasha commonly brings energy, initiative, and sometimes conflict or competition - themes of courage, property, siblings, and assertive action tend to surface, for better or worse depending on Mars's placement and strength.

Mercury Mahadasha is generally associated with communication, learning, business, and intellectual pursuits - a period often marked by increased mental activity, negotiations, travel, and commercial dealings.

Jupiter Mahadasha is traditionally considered one of the most favorable periods, associated with growth, wisdom, teachers, children, wealth, and good fortune broadly - though its exact expression still depends on Jupiter's placement and strength in the individual chart.

Venus Mahadasha commonly brings relationships, marriage, comfort, luxury, art, and pleasure to the forefront - it is one of the longest mahadashas (20 years) and often coincides with major romantic or material developments.

Saturn Mahadasha is associated with discipline, hard work, delay, and long-term structure. It has a reputation for being a challenging period, but classical texts also describe it as a time of durable, lasting achievement built through sustained effort rather than sudden gain.

Rahu Mahadasha is linked to ambition, unconventional paths, foreign connections, and sudden or unexpected change. It can bring rapid material gain or significant upheaval, and its results are considered strongly dependent on Rahu's house placement and the planets it associates with.

Ketu Mahadasha is associated with detachment, introspection, spirituality, and the release of old patterns. It is often described as a quieter, more internally-focused period compared to Rahu, sometimes bringing loss in worldly terms but growth in spiritual or introspective terms.

Nakshatras are the 27 lunar mansions the zodiac is divided into, each spanning 13 degrees 20 minutes. Each nakshatra has a ruling planet (used for Vimshottari Dasha calculations), a symbol, and a set of classical characteristics used to describe personality and inclination in more granular detail than the 12 rashis alone.

Nadi Dosha occurs in Ashtakoot marriage matching when both partners share the same Nadi (one of three groups: Aadi, Madhya, or Antya) based on their Moon's nakshatra. It is traditionally considered one of the most significant compatibility concerns, historically associated with concerns about the couple's health and offspring, and is often given more weight than the overall point total.

Mangal Dosha (also called Kuja Dosha) is said to occur when Mars is placed in the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th, or 12th house from the Ascendant. Classical texts suggest that when both partners in a match have Mangal Dosha, the affliction is considered to cancel out between them, which is why compatibility is usually checked for both people together rather than either one in isolation.

Panchanga refers to the five daily elements used in Vedic timekeeping: Tithi (lunar day, based on the angle between Sun and Moon), Vara (weekday), Nakshatra (the Moon's lunar mansion for the day), Yoga (a combined Sun-Moon angular measure), and Karana (half of a tithi). Together they form the traditional Hindu almanac used for both daily reference and birth-time analysis.
